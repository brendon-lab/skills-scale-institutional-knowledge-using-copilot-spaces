# OctoAcme — Project Management Overview

OctoAcme runs projects using an iterative, outcomes‑focused approach grounded in clear artifacts and decision gates. Work begins with a lightweight Project One‑pager that captures the problem, objective, and measurable success metrics. Once approved, initiatives move into planning where work is broken into prioritized backlog items with acceptance criteria, a Definition of Done, and an initial release timeline. The project lifecycle follows a simple flow: Initiation → Planning → Execution → Release → Close & Retrospective.

Day‑to‑day delivery emphasizes small, testable increments and predictable handoffs. Teams manage work on a project board (Backlog, Ready, In Progress, In Review, QA, Done) and follow a Pull Request workflow that favors small PRs, links PRs to issues and acceptance criteria, and requires CI checks and approvals prior to merging. Regular rhythms — daily standups for progress and blockers, weekly delivery syncs for progress and risks, and demos at sprint or milestone ends — keep stakeholders aligned and visible.

Roles and responsibilities are clearly defined: Product Managers set outcomes and success metrics, Project Managers coordinate schedules, risks, and communications, Developers implement and test code, and QA validates acceptance criteria. These personas help ensure clear ownership for planning, execution, and follow‑through. Retrospectives occur after sprints, releases, or incidents, and prioritized action items are tracked back into the backlog with owners and due dates.

Quality assurance and risk management are embedded into the process. QA practices include unit and integration tests, end‑to‑end smoke checks for critical flows, security scans in CI, and manual QA when needed. Release readiness requires passing CI/security scans, drafted release notes, a rollback plan, and smoke tests in staging before production deployment. A concise Risk Register captures impact, likelihood, owner, and mitigation and is reviewed regularly with escalation paths defined for critical issues.
